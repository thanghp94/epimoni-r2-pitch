# Epimoni MicroQuest — Round 2 rehearsal script

Official 13-section structure · 16 slides · no appendix · ~1,080 spoken words ≈ 9:00–9:50

## S1 · TITLE —   ·  DN  ·  0:35
*Cue:* Let the lines type. Speak slowly — you are the user, that is your advantage.
- It is 9pm in Hanoi. Minh has a science test tomorrow — forty pages of notes.
- In the next tab, ChatGPT will do all of it for him. At the table, Mom is quizzing him — with four apps that do not help.
- Minh is not lazy. Minh is us. Mom is doing her best.
- These tools are teaching him to stop thinking. We built the fix for both of them.

## S2 · PROBLEM 1/2 — Two struggles at one table.  ·  DN  ·  0:45
*Cue:* Left card is the kid. Right card is Mom. Land the doubt line at the bottom.
- Look at one dinner table. Two struggles.
- Left: Minh. Test tomorrow — and ChatGPT answers in one second. But did he learn anything? Nobody knows. That doubt is the problem.
- Right: Mom. Four paid apps, none connected. She still quizzes him by hand — paying more every year for less.
- Instant answers look fast — but they leave a doubt no parent can answer: is this real, or borrowed?

## S3 · PROBLEM 2/2 — Top marks. Empty learning.  ·  DN  ·  0:50
*Cue:* Walk the 2×2, land on the Paradox Zone, click it to reveal the three costs.
- Here is the strange part. On paper, Minh is a top student.
- Grades go right, real learning goes up. Bottom right: the Paradox Zone — top marks, drilled, brittle. That is where Minh lives. [click]
- Three invisible costs: burnout nobody sees, practice that breaks when the problem changes, and AI-dependence.
- Kids do not need more content. They need to want it — and know how to chase it.

## S4 · SOLUTION 1/2 —   ·  GM  ·  0:50
*Cue:* The countdown starts now and keeps running through the next slides — point at it.
- So the real question is not how to give better answers. It is how to protect the question.
- Our answer: we do not give answers. We run five-minute quests.
- One quest. One goal. Five minutes. Not a compromise — one complete rep.
- Three promises to a parent: learn faster, one real rep a day. Less time, five minutes then done. Move ahead faster, every quest levels up.

## S5 · SOLUTION 2/2 — Ask for the answer. Watch the shield.  ·  GM  ·  1:35
*Cue:* Same question, two windows. Let the chat play. MATH tab top-left if a judge asks about other subjects.
- Do not take my word for it. Same question. Two futures.
- Left: generic AI — full answer in under a second. Struggle skipped. Learning unverified.
- Right: Epimoni. He begs for the answer. The Shield gives a hint, then a guiding question — never the answer.
- He tries again and gets it himself. The reward is for the struggle, not the click.
- Below: rep, XP, character, gear, tomorrow's quest — his level is his learning.

## S6 · MARKET OPPORTUNITY — Learning apps are big. At home, they sit unused.  ·  TC  ·  0:45
*Cue:* Every number is an estimate — say so. The point is paid for ≠ learned.
- Now the market. Learning apps are big business. Quizizz earns about thirty million dollars a year. Kahoot, about a hundred ninety million. IXL has seventeen million students.
- Together with tutors and AI tools — more than a billion dollars a year.
- But at home, the tablets collect dust. Four apps, four logins, nobody connecting them. Mom becomes the system, every night.
- Paid for is not the same as learned.

## S7 · TARGET MARKET — Who pays first.  ·  TC  ·  0:50
*Cue:* Walk the funnel top to bottom. Numbers labeled est.
- So who pays first? Hungry parents.
- [walk funnel] Sixteen million K-12 students. Private, bilingual, homeschool — three to five hundred thousand. Parent groups — twenty to fifty thousand. First stop: one to two hundred families.
- If one in a hundred joins: three to five thousand subscribers, seven to twelve billion dong a year. Estimated.
- Vietnamese families already spend a fifth of their income on education — the highest in the region.

## S8 · MARKETING STRATEGY — Real students sell it. Not ads.  ·  TC  ·  0:50
*Cue:* Three channels, zero ad spend. This is a scored section — say the budget line clearly.
- How do we reach them without ads? Students.
- One: our pilot kids are the influencers — real before-and-after clips, top marks without suffering. Not actors.
- Two: parent ambassadors. Bring another family, get a free month — inside the homeschool groups where parents already talk.
- Three: a weekly community quest and a public leaderboard. The screenshots are our marketing.
- Marketing budget: about zero dollars. Our cost is time — three hours a week.

## S9 · SALES STRATEGY — Win where ambitious kids already compete.  ·  LH  ·  0:55
*Cue:* Three motions: competition seats, 5-year plan, bundles. Deliver the pricing clearly.
- We sell where ambitious kids already are: competitions.
- We sponsor quest packs for finalists at Math, Science and English competitions — Fermat, Geniusstar Vietnam and similar. A scholarship and a sales funnel in one.
- Then the five-year founding plan: ten million dong once — about four hundred dollars — instead of paying month by month.
- Bundles: one subject ninety-nine thousand a month; family plan a hundred ninety-nine.

## S10 · REVENUE STREAMS — One plan replaces the pile.  ·  LH  ·  0:50
*Cue:* Deliver the stack math cold — this is a scored section.
- Today a family pays forty to eighty dollars a month across four or more apps — and Mom connects it by hand.
- Epimoni is one family plan: about a hundred ninety-nine thousand VND a month. Every subject, every kid, one login.
- One quest costs us a few cents of AI. That is why the price works.
- Year one: a thousand families is about two point four billion dong. Estimated.

## S11 · COMPETITION — We connect the pile — we do not out-content it.  ·  GM  ·  0:45
*Cue:* Read the table down the rows. The last row is the honest one — say it with confidence.
- Khan has the lessons — we coach the struggle through them. IXL drills what you know — we fix why you are stuck. Duolingo rewards showing up — we reward thinking hard. ChatGPT hands over the answer — we hand the next hint.
- And if a big company copies the code? The hard part is not code — it is our teacher-checked lists of wrong ideas, and our pilot proof.

## S12 · CLIENT RETENTION — Why kids come back tomorrow.  ·  GM  ·  0:45
*Cue:* Left: the come-back loop. Right: the three retention facts. This is a scored section.
- The loop: one rep, Struggle XP, character, gear — and tomorrow's quest. No pay-to-skip, no streak punishment. Kids come back because it ends.
- Quests run Grade 3 to Grade 9 — finishing a grade unlocks the next. Every school year is a renewal, not a goodbye.
- One plan covers more subjects and more kids. The average family stays two and a half years — seven million dong — and arrives through a referral.

## S14 · 5-YEAR FORECAST — Small and honest. Then steady.  ·  LH  ·  0:50
*Cue:* Walk the bars left to right. Say "estimates" out loud.
- Our five-year forecast is small and honest.

- Then three thousand families, seven thousand, twelve thousand, eighteen thousand — about forty-five billion dong by year five. Growth slows on purpose; we are four students.
- Costs grow with content, not headcount.

- Remember Minh? Tonight he opens a different tab — one that hands him a quest, not answers. And Mom keeps one app, not four.
- In a world of free answers, we coach the struggle that makes learning stick. Thank you — questions welcome.
